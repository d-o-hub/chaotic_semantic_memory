# PR Roast — 2026-09-24

One open PR at session start: **#763** `perf(retrieval): defer score scaling in
single list hybrid merge` (Jules, draft, +66/−70, one file:
`crates/csm-retrieval/src/hybrid.rs`). CI green at the time of review.
Verdict: **roasted — correct, but the perf claim is unsubstantiated; requesting
evidence, not closing as no-op.**

## What is right

The central claim — that min-max scaling is strictly monotonic, so top-k
selection on raw scores picks the same subset — **holds**. Probed directly with
an equivalence harness over 186 (N, k) cases including exact ties, negative
scores and duplicated values:

```
cases=186 subset_mismatches=0
cases=90  value_mismatches=0  order_mismatches=0  worst_abs_diff=0e0
```

Returned scores are **bit-identical** to the current implementation, not merely
the same ordering. The preconditions are also genuinely satisfied: the caller
fast-paths only when the other list is empty, `compute_weights` returns strictly
positive weights (0.1–0.9), so `weight / range > 0` and the transform is
order-preserving in descending `total_cmp` order. The refactor is sound.

## Why the impact claim does not stand

The body claims *"Reduces floating-point calculations ... from O(N) to O(k),
saving N − k floating-point operations per retrieval operation."* The FLOP
saving is real by inspection, but the wall-clock effect is not. Throwaway
interleaved timing probe (A/B/A/B, `rustc -O`, 2000 reps × 2 rounds each,
`black_box` on the result), ns per call:

| N | k | old | new | delta |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 10 | 10 | 1 097 | 1 384 | **+26.2 %** |
| 100 | 10 | 2 283 | 2 127 | −6.8 % |
| 100 | 100 | 14 074 | 13 575 | −3.5 % |
| 1 000 | 10 | 12 378 | 9 869 | **−20.3 %** |
| 1 000 | 100 | 26 206 | 27 822 | +6.2 % |
| 10 000 | 10 | 130 463 | 145 266 | +11.3 % |
| 10 000 | 100 | 140 583 | 139 213 | −1.0 % |

Deltas swing across ±25 % **with no consistent sign**, and the two largest
"wins" sit at k/N ratios where the scaling is a minority of the work. The
dominant cost in this function is not the arithmetic — it is the
`select_nth_unstable_by` partition plus the `String` allocation per retained
element, both of which the diff leaves untouched. The N=10/k=10 regression is
the clearest signal: with k == N the partition is skipped entirely, so the diff
pays an extra `select_nth`-worth of work for nothing.

Note the comparison to the #737/#739/#740/#754 class: those were **wrong** or
provably zero-delta. This one is a genuine, if small, FLOP reduction that the
host cannot resolve above noise. Different verdict, different bar.

## Recommendations (in priority order)

1. **Gate the parity claim with a test.** The equivalence is a property of the
   code's correctness, not of the bench. One test asserting that
   `merge_single_list` output equals the scaled-then-selected baseline across
   (ties, negatives, k==N, k==1) would lock the property permanently and is
   worth more than the timing. **The PR adds no test** — that is the main
   substantive gap.
2. **Fix the `k == N` regression** by short-circuiting the partition when
   `k >= n`, or by only deferring scaling when `k * 4 < n` where the saving
   clearly dominates.
3. **Soften the body claim** to what the measurement supports: "N − k fewer
   float ops; wall-clock effect unproven above noise at these N".
4. **Re-measure on the named runner** before merging, not on a shared/loaded
   host — a ±25 % sign-flipping delta is exactly the condition the runner
   profile exists to avoid.
