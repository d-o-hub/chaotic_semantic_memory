## What
Introduces `FlyHash`, a locality-sensitive hash based on sparse random projections and winner-take-all mechanics, modeled after the Drosophila olfactory circuit. It is exposed in `crates/csm-chaos/src/hashing/fly_hash.rs` behind the feature flag `experimental-fly-hash`.

## Research basis
Link: [FlyHash for LLM Agent Memory: An Empirical Validation and a New Application](https://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05791v1) (Mohan Bheemadolu, Chrisogonas O. Odhiambo, 2026-11-04)

## Design
The implementation constructs a `10240 x input_dim` expansion projection matrix. The matrix is intentionally sparse, governed by a `sparsity` parameter (e.g., 0.1 to 0.5 probability of a non-zero element) to mimic biological sparse projections. Instead of Gaussian random sampling, we use the `Slhm2d` hyperchaotic map to deterministically generate both the sparsity mask and the non-zero projection weights for consistency with the rest of the repository. After projecting an `input_dim` dimensional embedding into 10240 dense activations, a winner-take-all operation activates only the `top_k` elements, converting them into a binary hash stored as a `[u64; 160]`.

It perfectly fits the existing structure alongside `ChaoticLsh` and `VoronoiLsh`, adhering to `validate_input_dim` limits and `MAX_INPUT_DIM` (4096).

## Tradeoffs
**Benefits**: Offers competitive retrieval capabilities, explicitly supporting sparse representation matching.
**Limitations**: The winner-take-all step incurs a sorting and sparse population cost, taking roughly 2x latency compared to a standard dot-product based parity `ChaoticLsh`.
**Numerical Risks**: High sparsity constraints must be balanced since our uniform mapping from `Slhm2d` may exhibit slight distribution bias.
